# Open replicas: the Laya family

> **TL;DR** Laya is an open replica, **not Jev**: its own weights, its own calibration, and Jev-tuned thresholds do not transfer. This page holds the Laya family (servers, ports, fine-tunes, workbenches) with each author's numbers; the which-is-which table and trust notes stay on [[ideas/open-replicas]], and the Warnings block on [[ideas/warnings]].

## Laya and its servers

Kitsuno (@GTurkawka) runs a fine-tuned Laya in shadow next to production Jev, trained on frontier-model labels and never on Jev answers; it does not decide yet. Numbers and caveats: [[ideas/builds-business]] (Kitsuno row).

In a seven-system stress test Laya was the most order-sensitive and its calibration collapsed on long option lists (decision-models-under-pressure, [[ideas/head-to-head-benchmarks]]).

Laya ([NandhaKishorM/laya](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/laya), Nandakishor M; `pip install laya`; 18593★ · 2026-09-23 · Apache-2.0): three encoder checkpoints plus a router, `laya-serve`; 32.8 ms per question on a T4. Its Jev figures are quoted, not measured; Jev leads Banking77 0.870 (quoted, 72 labels) vs 0.425 (77 labels, default 256-token option budget). **Independent caveats:** zero-shot on typed decisions the base checkpoints score 0.362 (English) and 0.352 (multilingual; 0.342 in Laya's own table), below Laya's 0.461 per-question majority-class baseline (the dataset card's own scored majority baseline is 0.520, a different measure) (Laya's model card, quoted by arbiter; the 0.766 belongs to the checkpoint fine-tuned on that benchmark); the English checkpoint scores 0.000 on Khmer at 0.952 confidence and 0.100 on 20-way Hindi intent (chance 0.050) while staying confident (arbiter); lev's encoders reach 61–67% on authored144 (SemIf's set; lev calls it von's); Laya Vision 0.689 vs Glance 0.886. JevBench v1.4.2 #41, 30.25.

On keltokhy's jgrep benchmarks ([local-models report](https://github.com/keltokhy/jgrep/blob/c4d929a0577425f1fe24bf2bd87de319855ac41e/docs/benchmarks/local-models-2026-09-22.md)): on 2026-09-21, with long records cropped, Laya got 115 and 100 of 150 right on what it kept; on 2026-09-22 its 512-token window refused all 60 long records (HTTP 422, 300 failed decisions). Jev's side of that run: [[ideas/head-to-head-benchmarks]].

**techarm: Laya vs Jev on games and Japanese support messages** ([write-up](https://techarm.dev/posts/laya-vs-jev-prompt-design), [@techarmdev](https://x.com/techarmdev/status/2106946226562191823), 2026-10-04; Mac Studio M1 Ultra; code in techarm/blog-code-examples, not captured). Median per decision: Laya's unofficial MLX port 10.0 ms (PyTorch 21.3 ms; the port matched it on 200 of 200 Snake states) vs Jev 178-238 ms over the API from Osaka, network included (~18×). Maze, 60 single forks where the one unvisited path is right: Laya's three checkpoints 16.7-48.3% (chance 49.7%); it ignored facts written only in the option descriptions and matched words between `state` and options. With per-direction facts moved into `state` and worded "never been there": multilingual 98.3% vs Jev 100% (85.0% with the earlier wording); typed-decisions 0%. Played through 20 mazes, Laya chose right at 49 of 103 mixed forks (47.6%) vs Jev 86 of 86: the 60-item set held no case where "up" was the answer, and Laya missed most of those (17 of 69 right); dropping the goal direction from `state` raised it to 78.1%. Snake, 80 states with one apple-ward move: Laya **12.5% → 100%** by rewording `state` and instructions alone (other wordings 8.8%, 6.2%; chance 34.4%); Jev 80 of 80 even on the 6.2% wording. **One added rule broke an existing one for both models**: with "Never choose trap." added beside "Never choose blocked.", fatal moves per 1,000 decisions went 0.0% → 2.3% for Jev (apples 64.1 → 49.0) and 0.4% → 2.2% for Laya; marking trap squares `blocked` instead kept Jev at 0.3%. 16 English-Japanese message pairs (same verdict in both languages): Jev 16 of 16 with English questions, 14 of 16 with Japanese; Laya at best 12 of 16, agreeing with Jev on about half; Laya labelled Japanese `language: None`. Author: Laya for fast repeated game or interface moves, Jev for text classification where a mistake costs. `unverified`; one author, small sets.

**John Mark: fine-tuned Laya vs Jev on toxic comments** ([write-up](https://johnmark.dev/blog/laya-vs-jev-toxicity-fine-tune), updated 2026-10-09; [@grc_john](https://x.com/grc_john/status/2108446993295323459); [scripts, test set and every answer](https://github.com/jmgb27/laya-vs-jev-toxicity)). Setup: 2,000 Civil Comments test comments, 500 from each of four rater-share bands, so far more toxic ones than the collection's mix; seven yes/no questions (toxic, severely toxic, obscene, threat, insult, identity attack, sexually explicit) sent to both models as the same `state` plus seven Noul questions in one request; Jev zero-shot (`typesafe/jev-1.13` via OpenRouter), Laya fine-tuned about two hours on an RTX 4090 on 20,000 training comments from the same collection plus 14,000 AG News questions. Metric: all seven answers match the raters' majority (a 50/50 split counts as yes), a probability of 0.5 or more counting as yes. Author's numbers: Jev **27%**, Laya out of the box 61%, fine-tuned Laya **83%**, a judge that always answers "fine" 71%. With each model's cut-off tuned per question on one half and scored on the other: Jev 71% (a tie with "always fine"), Laya 79%, fine-tuned 81%. Most of Jev's gap is saying yes too often: on "toxic" it caught 80% of the 567 majority-toxic comments (fine-tuned Laya 68%), but only 46% of its flags were toxic (Laya 83%). Reweighted to the collection's real mix: "always fine" ~92%, fine-tuned Laya ~93.5%, Jev ~43%. Rewording the questions lifted Jev to 37% at face value, 70% tuned. Correlation with the raters' share, averaged over the seven questions: fine-tuned Laya 0.74, base 0.51, Jev 0.49. Median for seven questions: Laya 30 ms on the GPU, Jev 274 ms through OpenRouter (100 one-at-a-time requests); Jev cost about 8 cents in all. The first, toxicity-only fine-tune broke Laya's other skills (AG News topics 95% → 4%, emotions 57% → 7%); mixing AG News back in restored news (94%) but not emotions (37%). Author's caveats: the fine-tune practised on the same collection and bands, no simple-classifier or Detoxify baseline, one training run each. Our read: this is zero-shot Jev at a fixed 0.5 cut-off against a model trained on the test's own distribution; Noul answers are probabilities to threshold on your own labels ([[concepts/noul]], [[concepts/confidence]]) (inferred). Several Nouls in one request `verified` ([[concepts/primitives]]); scores `unverified` (author's run; data and answers public).

In a roleplay-router bake-off Laya multilingual answered the same route on all 90 attempts and the English checkpoint did worse (numbers: [[ideas/head-to-head-agents]], Dmytro Sichkar's row).

Laya multilingual (offline, `laya==0.3.20`) in a Persian benchmark beside Jev, Jeff and Mizan, with Laya the weakest of the five on general Persian questions ([Jev-Persian-Benchmark](https://github.com/ArmanJR/Jev-Persian-Benchmark), ArmanJR; numbers: [[ideas/head-to-head-benchmarks]]).

Labelled "JEV FREE": [@danjindev](https://x.com/danjindev/status/2111106115383537724) (2026-10-16) tests "laya(JEV FREE)" against OpenRouter free tiers on lead qualification; that is Laya, not a free Jev (no numbers in the post text; image not transcribed; [[ideas/warnings]] (e)).

reflex-router measured how often its Laya backend (with a calibration head fitted to Jev) and TypeLLM agree with Jev's routing plans: [[ideas/tools-agent-routing]].

**Chinese-Jev** ([arXiv 2609.36965](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36965), Fudan University, NUS and UCAS; [project](https://gulucaptain.github.io/Chinese-Jev/)): a ~322M encoder starting from Laya Multilingual, trained on ~10M Chinese decisions, then separate medical, legal and financial fine-tunes; CJ-Bench is the authors' own held-out set. Paper: general accuracy 40.22% (Laya Multilingual) → 69.20%, 1.24% (relative) above Jev, ECE 3.78%, 20.3× faster; medicine +4.0% over Jev, 92% of Jev's average across the specialist domains at ~15 ms; INT8 on a phone ~1 s a decision. The paper says models and data "will" be released (none linked in the capture). `unverified`

**Vega** ([write-up](https://www.nandakishorm.com/writing/vega); [vegaml](https://github.com/NandhaKishorM/vegaml), 92★ · 2026-10-10 · Apache-2.0; [@Nandakishorm1](https://x.com/Nandakishorm1/status/2108824179122618669), 2026-10-10) is **not Jev and not Laya**: a new open "typed decision model" from Laya's author. A frozen Qwen3.5-0.8B (or a 4B) reads one prompt; a small trained engine (14.3M parameters, about 55–57 MB; 30.9M for the 4B) picks the answer by rolling a simulated ball into one valley per candidate answer, and returns a calibrated probability, a conformal answer set and an abstain flag; 73,728-token context; images through the backbone's frozen vision encoder; optional per-task adapters behind a per-question gate; a Python library (`pip install vegaml`), no server (author). Author's runs against live Jev 1.13.0 on identical items, no tuning on the evaluation data, on tasks the author picked because Vega leads there (the author says Jev leads on most others, notably reranking and multi-step reasoning over long documents): phishing screening, 800 emails, 75.4 vs 61.9 (caught 252 vs 99 of 400); dates and quantities 46.7 vs 20.0; enron-spam 1.000 vs 0.920; AG News 0.955 vs 0.806; typed-decisions 0.438 vs 0.395; calibration error 6.0 vs 22.0 on product relevance, 9.5 vs 9.3 overall (5,096 paired); median 267 ms (T4 fp16) vs 591 ms hosted. Images: on RVL-CDIP-N (1,002 pages, 12 of 16 classes) Apple Vision OCR then Jev 0.896 (ECE 0.045, 1,049 ms with 649 ms of OCR) vs Vega reading the image zero-shot 0.793 (ECE 0.207); on MMMU-Pro (300 items) Jev on text only 0.337 vs Vega with the image 0.240. 0.8B vs 4B on 2,050 decisions: soft accuracy 0.680 vs 0.725, 22 vs 28 ms. Jev's text-only input `verified` ([[reference/models-and-pricing]]); scores `unverified` (author's runs, a selected set).

Names only (C, not captured; our check): laya-mps (Apple MPS; its own `/v1/decisions`, not a drop-in), sys1 (Rust server, entropy confidence), laya-server (1Panel; own keys and admin UI), **ollaya** (an Ollama look-alike in name, CLI and port, its FAQ says unaffiliated; installed by `curl | sh` that adds a systemd service; TanStack AI's evaluate docs list an ollaya adapter since 2026-09-30, [[ideas/framework-integrations]]; Warnings (e) on [[ideas/warnings]]), **deepopen** (loads Laya's weights and repeats Laya's figures; its pyproject names Convai Innovations, Laya's Hugging Face publisher, as author, relationship unconfirmed; no measurements of its own against Jev; PyPI `deepopen` does not exist), laya-jev-GraphRAG (its Jev client reads the wrong response fields and hides errors; distillation: [[ideas/warnings]] (b)).

**Laya 0.4.1 vs Jev 1.13, same items** ([jev-measured](https://github.com/WallerChen/jev-measured) §9, WallerChen, 2026-10-09; English checkpoint zero-shot on an Apple M2, Jev via OpenRouter, one question per call): Laya / Jev accuracy AG News **91.0** / 88.0%, Emotion 60.0 / 63.0, support tickets 74.1 / 100, MASSIVE (59 options) 45.0 / 76.0, Banking77 (77) 44.0 / 82.0, WikiToxic 91.0 / 92.0, Amazon polarity 67.0 / 98.0; wrong at ≥ 90% confidence (Choice) 26.8 / 11.5%; median 163 ms on the laptop vs 184 ms via OpenRouter. Laya's `confidence` was recomputed on Jev's formula for the comparison; Laya's own README advises under ~20 options (shared 192-token option budget). One run, `unverified`. The author's 13-model run: [[ideas/head-to-head-rival-models]].

**Fine-tuned Laya vs Jev, task unstated** ([@its_d_i_m_a](https://x.com/its_d_i_m_a/status/2109285279765049614), 2026-10-11): after fine-tuning Laya, @its_d_i_m_a reports 25.28% for Laya vs 39.62% for Jev and praises Laya's speed; the post states no task, dataset, metric, sample size or fine-tuning setup, and no write-up is linked. `unverified`

**Fine-tuned Laya first, Jev as fallback** ([@Carlami25552231](https://x.com/Carlami25552231/status/2114348755889914079), 2026-10-25): the author runs a tuned, gated Laya as their main local decision model and calls Jev only when Laya cannot handle a task. They report stock Laya handles "about 2/3 of 5 tasks that Jev would normally do" (ambiguous as written) and tuning brings it to 4 of 5, locally, near instant and free; their tuned LFM 2.5 3B (Liquid AI's model, not Jev) is the local vision model that blanks personal details in images and documents before anything goes to an API model. Neither model was released at capture (the author was still checking licences before a GitHub release). `unverified`: post only, no tasks, data or numbers beyond the counts.
